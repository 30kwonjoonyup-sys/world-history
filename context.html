Historical Context

Climate of the Korean Peninsula

The Korean Peninsula experienced dramatic temperature changes between summer and winter.

  • In winter, temperatures dropped to about -6°C to 3°C
  • In summer, temperatures rose to about 23°C to 30°C
  • Ancient Koreans faced harsh and long-lasting cold temperatures during winter

17th Century Catalyst

  • Around 17th century when the period of unusually low temperature, known as Little Ice Age peaked, the temperature in the winter of Korean peninsula became unbearable for the citizens
    • Indicates the decrease of efficiency of conventional furnaces and the increase in demand for fuels
  • “Chung [44] argued that the excessive level of pine needles noted in Seong’s publications was because low temperatures during the Little Ice Age limited the nutrient recycling of organic matters”(Bae and Kim).
  • Using the plenty of pine needles that did not decompose, people could support the fuels of Ondol, and later, government officially spurred the expansion
  • Furthermore, countless invasions from Japan and China devastated and razed the villages which paved a way for Ondol’s expansion and adoption
  • Ondol’s effectiveness in heat delivery enabled people to stay in one place while being able to eat, study, and sleep, and the reason for it is not arbitrary, but rather specifically intended with the ideal structure
    • The source of Ondol’s heat derives from the Agungi, a place where the fuel is burnt, and the amount of heat is adjustable
    • Once the air is heated, it enters Gorae, an extremely complex maze for the airway, tailored to retain the heat as long as possible, as the hot air is impossible to escape directly.
    • The chimney, comprised of the Yeonga, the specialized Korean traditional brick that constitutes the outer layer of the chimney, releases the hot air into the exterior.

Historical Evolution of Ondol

But before the Ondol was built, people in the upper class used furnace, but at that time, people in the lower class could not use the furnace. Later on, during the Goguryeo Kingdom, the common people first used and spread Ondol for their survival during the cold winters in Korea. Then the Ondol started to spread to even the nobles (who were already using the Hwaro) during the Joseon Kingdom, and new customs like sitting on floors while eating began to form.

  • Ondol formed some customs that is specially used by Koreans
  • The exact inventor of Ondol is unknown, and its origins remain somewhat mysterious
  • The Ondol developed as time passed… There are 2 different types of Ondol
    • The first was 쪽구들. 쪽구들 forms heat from 아궁이, and as the fire from the 아궁이 forms smoke and as the smoke moves through the small pathway underneath the floor, it forms heat. But because it is the first type of Ondol built, it only heated parts of the house.
    • Second was 구들. It forms heat in the same way as 쪽구들, but it heats the whole room instead of heating only some part of the room.

Innovation

  • Before the invention of Ondol, furnace was used as main heating system
  • Furnace’s heat didn’t last long, and the heat was unevenly distributed throughout the door
  • Ondol was a heating system that allowed one fire to warm the entire house.
  • It allowed heat to last for a long time and remain steady.
  • Ondol was widely used across different social classes, including both lower- and higher-class Koreans
  • Because of Ondol, Koreans were able to have a more comfortable lifestyle during winter
  • Also due to Ondol, Korean people started to live in floors; this was the start of Korean’s 좌식생활 in which people do their tasks while sitting down on the floor
